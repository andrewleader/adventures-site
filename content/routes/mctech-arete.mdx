## Overview

**McTech Arete** is a **6-pitch trad rock climb** on **Crescent Spire** in the Bugaboos, with an overall grade of **5.10-**. It is widely regarded as a **classic**, especially for **splitter granite crack climbing**, and is a popular **short-weather-window or rest-day alpine objective** because it is **close to Applebee Camp** and has **bolted rappel anchors**.

The best climbing is generally reported on **P1, P2, P4, and P5**, with **P2** and **P4** especially praised, and the **views from the top of P6** are fantastic!

**Important:** this route is **popular and often crowded**.

## Parking

Park at the [Conrad Kain Hut Trailhead](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Sya8p4qzQwRPkXxy8).

## Approach to camp

3.0 miles, 3,179 ft gain one-way, about 1 to 1.5 hours.

## Approach from camp to climb

About **45 minutes from Applebee Camp**, 0.8 miles, 388 ft gain one-way, but it's over loose boulders.

## Gear

- **Rope: Single 60 m rope** is sufficient for the rappels.
- **Protection:**
  - A practical average rack would be:
    - **1x #0.2, 1x #0.3**
    - **Triples in #0.4, #0.5, #0.75**
    - **2x #1**
    - **2–3x #2**
    - **1x #3**
    - **1x #4**
  - If you like to **sew up P2**, bring **extra finger-size cams**, especially **#0.4–#0.75**.
  - Several climbers specifically mention the **#4** being useful **multiple times**.
  - Nuts are optional; a few were placed by some parties, but most don't bring nuts.
- **Quickdraws/alpine draws:** 6-8 single alpine draws.
- **Crack gloves:** Useful.
- **Radio:** Not necessary.
- **Microspikes / ice axes:** Seasonally necessary (wasn't necessary in September 2026).

## Climb

### P1: 5.9, ~29m

Climb flakes to a **small corner roof**, then step left into another crack and continue up a **short chimney** to a belay on top of a pillar.

There is also a well-liked **direct roof variation** that many climbers prefer. This goes **straight up the roof crack** at about **5.10 / 5.10a** on **perfect hands**.

Build a **gear anchor on top of a pillar** (small and slghtly uncomfortable belay spot).

Alternatively, some climbers link **P1 and P2**.

![Direct roof variation of P1](https://leaderimages.blob.core.windows.net/images/large/0yR9oiWQCoAE.jpg)
![Top of P1](https://leaderimages.blob.core.windows.net/images/large/z3mLbdvjlWDu.jpg)

### P2: 5.10-, ~29m

A superb, sustained **finger-to-hand crack pitch** and one of the route's main attractions. From the belay, move **left** into the crack, climb to an **alcove**, then continue through it and jam the crack up the steep face to a **ledge with chain anchor**.

This pitch is repeatedly described as **classic**, **splitter**, and **sustained**, and it tends to **eat #0.5 and #0.75 cams**. Many parties recommend saving your finger-size gear for this pitch.

### P3: 5.4

An easier pitch heading up through corners and easier terrain to another anchor. It trends **up and right**. This is generally considered the **least memorable pitch** and more of a transition than a destination.

Belay at **chains**.

### P4: 5.9, ~40m

Step right from the anchor, go **behind a flake**, then climb a **beautiful corner** to a roof. Traverse **right under the roof** into another corner, pull the **second roof**, and finish at a **belay ledge on the left with chains**.

This pitch is one of the route highlights and was the **favorite pitch** for multiple climbers. Expect **excellent hand crack and corner climbing**, with a memorable roof sequence. A few climbers felt it climbed a bit **stiffer than 5.9**.

### P5: 5.8, 31m

Climb cracks on the face to the right, then gain a corner leading to another ledge with chains. This pitch is often praised as another **splitter granite** highlight.

### P6: 5.7, 35m

Climb easy terrain **toward the right**, then climb a **steep corner** to the top of the **ridge crest** and **belay off a large block with your own sling**.

This pitch requires a #3 and #4 to protect it well.

The climbing isn't that interesting, but the view from the top is highly worth it!

## Descent

**Estimated descent time:** about **1 hour rappelling** plus roughly **30 minutes back to camp**.

**Descent method:** Rappel using bolted anchors and a single 60m rope. The rappel route sometimes diverges from the climbing route.

### R1: 20m

From the very top of the ridge, find the bolted rappel anchor with chains around the edge. There's a big cairn to help indicate it, but you need to peer over the sheer edge to see the anchor.

When rappelling down, traverse far climbers right. Ignore the rap anchor straight down.

### R2: 30m

Straight down, no reason to traverse right. Watch for the rope getting stuck here.

### R3: 29m

Straight down again, to a non-route rappel anchor.

### R4: 28m

Straight down to bolted chains on a clean vertical face.

### R5: 28m

Down and then to the right. Ignore the anchor climbers left towards the corner gulley.

### R6: 21m

Down and right.

### R7: 22m

Straight down to the ground.

### Bonus rappels: bypassing the steep ascent trail

Two additional bolted rappels go straight down from the start of the climb, letting you skip the steep ascent trail entirely:

- **First rappel:** short, about 20m max.
- **Second rappel:** a full 30m, ending at the bottom edge of the snowfield where it's less steep.
